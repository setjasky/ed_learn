1. Dimensional Architecture of FSLSM

Learning in formal environments is conceptualized as a two-step sequence: Information Reception and Information Processing. Unlike rigid typologies (e.g., MBTI), FSLSM treats learning preferences as continuous, fluid traits that vary with instructional context.

Active ↔ Reflective

Processing Mode

Active learners absorb concepts through physical manipulation, peer discussions, and immediate trial-and-error experimentation. Reflective learners require solitary introspective cogitation to evaluate logical implications prior to taking action. Derived from Kolb's Experiential Learning Theory.

Sensing ↔ Intuitive

Perceptual Orientation

Sensors favor concrete facts, empirical data, structured procedures, and practical engineering applications. Intuitors seek abstract theories, mathematical formulations, non-linear patterns, and creative innovation. Based on Jungian Psychological Types.

Visual ↔ Verbal

Sensory Input Modality

Visual learners retain spatial and visual representations such as flowcharts, schematics, plots, and visual demonstrations. Verbal learners excel with spoken discourse, written prose, and textual descriptions. Supported by Dual-Coding Theory.

Sequential ↔ Global

Cognitive Organization

Sequential learners master concepts in linear, step-by-step incremental progressions. Global learners absorb information non-linearly, experiencing sudden holistic breakthroughs that enable complex systems-level synthesis.

2. Longitudinal Model Evolution & Refinements

In 2002, Richard Felder introduced two structural modifications to the original 1988 framework based on cognitive science advances and practical educational considerations.

REMOVING DEDUCTIVE BIAS Excised Dimension

Excision of Inductive / Deductive Dimension

The original 1988 model included an Inductive vs. Deductive dimension. Felder removed it in 2002 to prevent a pedagogical trap: traditional professors observed student preferences for deductive lectures and used it to justify continued passive teaching.

Why Inductive Teaching Supercedes Preference:

Educational research proves that inductive methods (problem-based, inquiry-based learning) produce superior long-term conceptual retention and critical thinking for all student cohorts, regardless of stated preference.

COGNITIVE PHONOLOGY Refined Modality

Shift from Visual/Auditory to Visual/Verbal

In 1988, text was ambiguously placed. In 2002, Felder reclassified written prose into the Verbal pole alongside spoken words, reserving the Visual pole strictly for non-verbal graphical representations.

Working Memory Neural Mechanism:

Cognitive science proves the brain converts written characters into phonological codes in working memory. Text is processed via auditory neural pathways, not iconic visual pathways.

3. Psychometric Evaluation of the Index of Learning Styles (ILS)

Developed in 1991 (revised 1994) by Felder and Soloman, the 44-item forced-choice ILS instrument yields bounded preference scores $S_d \in \{-11, -9, \dots, +11\}$.

4. STEM Pedagogical Mismatch & Instructional Balance

A major systemic gap exists in higher engineering education: while traditional STEM courses rely heavily on passive lecture formats, the majority of undergraduate students favor active, empirical, visual, and holistic learning modes.

Teaching Delivery vs. Student Preference Gap

STEM Engineering Cohorts

Percentage bias toward left-pole learning modalities: STEM Instructors (Lecture Mode) vs. STEM Undergraduate Population.

Pedagogical Risk: Mismatches lead to low student self-efficacy, grade inflation suppression, and high attrition rates among capable practical (Sensing) and creative holistic (Global) learners.

Principles of Balanced Instruction

Felder explicitly rejects using FSLSM to track or sort students into rigid silos. Instead, courses must be designed to balance teaching techniques across both poles.

Active + Reflective Integration

Combine brief peer-problem solving pauses during lectures with solitary analytical writing prompts.

Sensing + Intuitive Integration

Anchor abstract mathematical derivations with physical engineering case studies and concrete laboratory data.

Visual + Verbal Integration

Supplement text heavy slides with interactive schematics, data flow plots, visual demonstrations, and visual charts.

Sequential + Global Integration

Provide macro-level system architecture previews before launching into detailed step-by-step derivations.

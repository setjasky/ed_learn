Theoretical Overview

Deconstructing Metacognitive Self-Regulated Learning

Philip H. Winne and Allyson F. Hadwin’s model conceptualizes studying as an inherent exercise in metacognitively governed information processing. Unlike holistic stage-based models, Winne and Hadwin provide a microanalytic, four-phase recursive framework governed by the **COPES** architecture (Conditions, Operations, Products, Evaluations, Standards) and powered by the **SMART** cognitive operational taxonomy.

Initial Cognitive Processing

In Phase 1, the learner processes available cues to construct an internal subjective mental model of the academic task. A crucial feature of Winne and Hadwin's model is isolating Task Definition as an explicit phase prior to planning; diagnostic learning failures often stem from misinterpreting rubric requirements rather than poor execution.

Interactive Module 2

COPES Cognitive Blueprint & SMART Operations

Within each phase, cognitive events are governed by **COPES** (the structural framework) and executed via **SMART** (the elementary cognitive operational taxonomy). Click components below to examine their operational mechanics.

Conditions (C) — Contextual Constraints & Internal Stores

Conditions comprise the baseline information resources and environmental parameters available to the learner. They are split into **Cognitive Conditions** (internal: prior domain knowledge, motivation, anxiety, self-efficacy, epistemic beliefs) and **Task Conditions** (external: instructional prompts, rubrics, time availability, environmental noise).

Academic Context Example

A student starting a biology term paper has high prior knowledge of genetics (Cognitive Condition) but faces a tight 48-hour deadline and a strict 10-citation rubric limit (Task Condition).

Searching (S) — Retrieval & Environmental Scanning

Searching involves inspecting external sources (e.g., scanning a textbook index, searching online databases) or searching internal long-term memory stores for specific information nodes, key concepts, or procedural steps.

Exemplar Execution in Learning

Scanning a textbook chapter for bolded vocabulary words or retrieving a calculus formula from memory during an exam.
